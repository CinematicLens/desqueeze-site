iPad — HDMI Monitor - Cine (CineMonitor)

CineMonitor is our web name for HDMI Monitor - Cine on the App Store — a professional iPad cinema and field monitor for filmmakers, camera assistants, DITs, and colorists. Connect camera HDMI through a UVC capture card to USB-C iPad Pro or iPad Air.

  • Capture: external HDMI/UVC live preview, resolution/FPS readout, hot-plug reconnect, audio meters
  • Focus & exposure: peaking, zebra, false color, 1:1 / 2× / 4× zoom loupe
  • Scopes: luma waveform, histogram, RGB parade, vectorscope — overlay or dedicated workspace
  • Color: import industry-standard .cube 3D LUTs, intensity control, instant A/B bypass
  • Composition: 1.85:1, 2.39:1, 4:3, 9:16, custom guides; still capture to Photos

General-purpose HDMI monitor — no anamorphic desqueeze on iPad. For live anamorphic de-squeeze, use CineMonitors on Android below or Anamorphic Desqueezer.

How to use HDMI Monitor - Cine on iPad

Set up a portable HDMI field monitor in a few minutes — camera HDMI in, live scopes and LUTs on iPad out.

Step 1
Connect your rig

Use a USB-C iPad Pro or iPad Air (iPadOS 17+). Run camera HDMI out → HDMI capture card (UVC class) → USB-C to iPad. Mount landscape on a c-stand arm or cage. Use a powered USB-C hub if the capture card needs extra bus power.

Step 2
Install & open the app

Download HDMI Monitor - Cine from the App Store. Plug in the capture device before or after launch — hot-plug reconnect is supported. Allow external video access if iPadOS prompts you. Confirm resolution, FPS, and audio meters on the live feed.

Step 3
Scopes, exposure & focus

Open waveform, histogram, RGB parade, or vectorscope. Enable zebra, false color, and focus peaking. Use the 1× / 2× / 4× loupe for critical focus checks without leaving the live feed.

Step 4
LUTs & framing

Import .cube LUTs, adjust intensity, and use A/B bypass. Add 1.85:1 or 2.39:1 guides and grab reference stills to Photos for directors or continuity.

  • Framelines, safe areas, grid, zebra, false color, focus peaking

How to use CineMonitors on Android

Two workflows — shoot anamorphic on the device camera, or monitor a cinema camera over HDMI/UVC.

Step 1
Connect or shoot

For native recording, open CineMonitors and use the device camera. For external monitor mode, connect camera HDMI out → HDMI-to-USB capture card → Android phone or tablet via USB-C / OTG.

Step 2
Set anamorphic ratio

Pick the squeeze factor that matches your lens (1.33×, 1.55×, 2×, etc.). Add framelines and safe areas so you see the final frame while shooting or monitoring.

Step 3
Monitor exposure & focus

Enable zebra, false color, and focus peaking. Rotate or flip the feed if your rig is inverted. Use clean feed mode when you only need a client monitor.

Step 4
Record or view

Record native anamorphic video on the phone, or stay in external monitor mode for on-set viewing. Results depend on device USB host support, capture hardware, and camera HDMI output.
